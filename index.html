Escalation map

If a written demand with a 15-day deadline gets you nothing, here is the ladder. Most disputes settle before the last rung.

1

Written demand (day 0)

Itemized breakdown demanded with tax invoices, 15-day deadline, name the forum you'll use next. This alone settles most cases.

2

Legal notice (day 16)

An advocate-drafted notice carries the most weight; a clear written demand from you is also valid. Jurisdiction: where the property is located, not where you moved. Three-year limitation.

3

Civil suit / Rent Authority

Civil suit for recovery of money where the property sits. Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Assam have direct Rent Authority routes; Karnataka practice is uneven, check current rules.

4

Consumer commission (e-Jagriti)

Filed on e-Jagriti, no fee up to ₹5 lakh, but whether a tenant counts as a "consumer" is contested, so it may decline. Get advice before choosing this route.

What not to do: don't file a police FIR first for a pure money dispute (they treat it as civil), don't post on social media before a notice (defamation risk, not leverage), never sign a full-and-final settlement under pressure, and claim interest and costs in whatever you file — most tenants forget both.
